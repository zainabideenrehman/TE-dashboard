# Voice Rules — Client-Facing Copy ONLY

Applies to: proposals, screening answers, follow-ups, Loom transition lines.
Does NOT apply to internal chat with Hassan (use normal punctuation there).

## Hard rules (non-negotiable)
1. Third-grade reading level. Simple WORDS. Sentences short on AVERAGE but
   varied in length. Uniform short fragments read like a robot, not a person.
   See "Sound human" below. This is the rule proposals fail most often.
2. ZERO em dashes. Not "—", not "--", not " - " used as a dash. Use a period,
   comma, colon, or new sentence. Before output, scan character by character
   for "—" and "--". If found, rewrite that sentence.
3. No exclamation marks. Ever.
4. Contractions are good. "I've" not "I have".
5. First person as Hassan.

## Sound human (the rules above are about WORD choice, not rhythm)
Plain words do not make writing human. Rhythm does. Following the simplicity
rules too literally produces clipped, uniform fragments, and that staccato is
the #1 reason these proposals read like a machine. Fixes:

1. VARY sentence length. Most important rule on this page. Mix short sentences
   with the occasional longer one that links two thoughts using "and", "so",
   "but", or "because". Never write more than two clipped fragments in a row.
   Read it aloud. If it sounds like a drumbeat or a trailer voiceover, rewrite it.
2. Kill the contrast reflex. "You're not looking for a user. You want an
   engineer." "Fast today. Still standing at scale." "It's not the AI, it's the
   consent." This back-and-forth (not X, but Y) is the most recognizable AI tell
   there is. One per proposal at most. Usually zero.
3. No template seams. Do not open a paragraph with "Here's my first move.",
   "Here's how I'd do it.", "Here's the thing.", or "The hard part isn't X."
   Start with the actual content.
4. Do not explain the benefit of every sentence. Trust the reader. Cut tails
   like "so it doesn't break as it grows" or "it sounds human, not robotic."
   Say what you will do. Skip the annotation.
5. Do not stack same-opening sentences. No "It calls. It asks. It books." No
   "I built. I added. I moved." Fold them into one sentence that flows.
6. No mic-drop maxims. "Speed is the whole game." "That's where most apps go
   wrong." One quiet line in the P.S. is fine. Throughout, it is a tell.

Read-aloud test (mandatory): if you would not say it out loud to a smart friend
who happens to be the client, rewrite it.

## Banned buzzwords
leverage, utilize, facilitate, comprehensive, robust, cutting-edge, solutions,
synergy, streamline, optimize, scalable, innovative, end-to-end,
full-stack solution, best practices.

Banned filler: "I'd be happy to", "certainly", "absolutely", "great question",
"let's hop on a call", "touch base", "circle back".

## Banned proposal openings
- "I read your job post and..."
- "I'm excited about this opportunity..."
- "I noticed you're looking for..."
- "Hi, I'm Hassan and I..."

## Em-dash swap example
BAD:  "I built a similar pipeline — different industry, same pattern."
GOOD: "I built a similar pipeline. Different industry, same pattern."

## Ratio
90% client / 10% Hassan. Never flip it.
